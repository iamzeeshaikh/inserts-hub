# Candle Box Inserts for Glass Jar Protection

A filled candle jar is a genuinely strange object to ship. It is heavy like a paperweight, fragile like a drinking glass, and top-heavy once the lid goes on tight. Drop one unprotected in a box and the physics decide the outcome quickly. Candle box inserts exist to referee that physics before the courier gets involved. The insert grips the jar firmly, spends the impact energy, and keeps printed labels clear of every wall. This guide covers measurement, restraint, material choice, weight math, and the drop tests that prove a design before a launch ships.

## Measure the candle container properly

Insert design starts with the vessel, measured the way it actually ships. Record the diameter at the base, at the widest point, and across the lid. Height needs two numbers, with the lid on and off. Five minutes with calipers saves weeks of revision later.

Candle vessels hide small surprises in their shapes. Many jars taper slightly toward the base, and tumbler styles flare at the mouth. Lids often overhang the glass by a millimeter or two on each side. Wooden lids and metal lids behave differently under pressure, so note the material too. If your line spans several vessels, measure each one rather than scaling. A single shared insert design almost always fits one jar well and the rest poorly.

## Restrain the base and the sides

A jar in transit wants to do two things, slide and tip. Good inserts answer both with separate features doing separate jobs. Neither feature can substitute for the other.

Base restraint comes from a fitted well or collar that grips the bottom third of the jar. That grip stops sliding and keeps the heavy base centered squarely over the box floor. Side restraint comes from walls or wings that stop the tip before it starts. Purpose-made [candle boxes with fitted inserts](/product/candle-boxes-with-inserts/) build both into one die-cut piece. The lid zone should stay clear, since pressure on a threaded lid transfers straight into the glass neck. A jar held at base and shoulder survives falls that a loose jar never could.

## Cardboard and foam candle box inserts compared

Candle programs usually choose between die-cut board and cut foam. Both work, and the right pick follows weight, volume, and brand positioning.

The tradeoffs sort cleanly into a short list:

- Board inserts fold flat, print cleanly, and recycle with the box
- Dense [custom foam insert cavities](/product/custom-foam-inserts/) absorb sharper shocks for heavy vessels
- Foam holds tighter tolerances around curved and tapered glass
- Board costs less per unit at typical candle volumes
- Foam suits gift sets where several jars share one cavity block
- Board matches natural-brand looks that foam undercuts

Most single-jar retail programs land on board without regret. Heavy multi-wick vessels and premium gift sets justify foam's extra cost through pure protection. Mixed approaches exist too, with a board shell around one foam base pad.

## Protect printed surfaces and labels

Candle branding lives on the glass, and abrasion is its quiet enemy. A label that rubs against board for three days arrives dull, creased, or peeled at the corners. The candle inside is fine, and the product is still ruined.

The insert solves this by holding the jar clear of every wall. Clearance of just a few millimeters around the label zone is enough. Contact points belong on bare glass at the base and shoulder, never mid-label. For frosted and painted vessels, add a tissue or glassine wrap before the jar ever meets the insert. Matte painted finishes scratch the most easily of all vessel types. One scuffed jar in an unboxing video costs more than a thousand liners.

## Account for the weight

Weight is the silent factor in candle packaging. A standard filled jar runs heavier than almost anything else its size in e-commerce parcels. Wax is dense, glass is dense, and together they surprise every courier scale.

That mass stresses every part of the packaging system at once. Insert walls must not crush under the jar during a corner drop. Box seams carry the full load when the parcel is lifted by one flap. Multi-jar sets multiply the numbers fast, and a three-jar gift set quickly approaches the weight of a small kitchen appliance. Board grade and [structural cardboard box inserts](/product/cardboard-box-inserts/) should be specified against the real shipped weight, not the vessel size. Ask your supplier to state the tested load for any multi-jar layout.

## Test drops and vibration honestly

Candle inserts earn their keep in the last few feet of delivery, at the doorstep drop. A short test sequence at the bench predicts that moment reliably before any order ships.

1. Pack a finished production candle exactly as a customer order ships.
2. Drop the packed parcel flat from waist height onto bare concrete.
3. Drop it again on each corner and inspect the glass and label.
4. Shake the box hard for a full minute to mimic truck vibration.
5. Stack three identical parcels overnight and check the bottom box carefully.
6. Open every test parcel fully and photograph all the results.

Wax adds a wrinkle worth testing in season. Summer heat softens wax, and a softened candle shifts weight inside its own jar. If you ship through hot months, leave a test parcel in a warm car for an afternoon. The insert should still hold the jar centered when the wax cools again.

Vibration deserves equal respect to drops in candle testing. Hours of road hum work jars loose in ways one impact never shows. The minute of hard shaking in the sequence above stands in for that slow loosening.

## Prepare the order specification

A candle insert order comes together from a short list of confirmed facts. Assemble them once, carefully, and every future reorder inherits the work for free.

State the vessel dimensions, lid type, and finished shipping weight in one document. Name the insert material and the exact box style it must fit. Reference your recorded drop test results and the exact sample version that passed them. Precision die-cutting programs like [custom die-cut insert designs](/product/die-cut-inserts/) can then reproduce the exact geometry run after run. Note any candle-specific handling, such as tissue wraps for painted vessels, directly in the written specification.
